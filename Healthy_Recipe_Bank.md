# Healthy Recipe Bank: Family of 3, Bangalore

For: two adults (M35 with hypertension, F34, both managing weight) and a toddler (2.5 yrs). Vegetarian plus eggs.
Equipment: stove, microwave, air fryer. Cook strengths: stuffed parathas, pulao, chapathis, fried rice.

**All nutrition numbers are estimates per adult serving** (rounded, from IFCT/standard food-table values, not lab-tested). Treat them as ±15%. Nutrition tables used: soya chunks ~345 kcal/52 g protein/13 g fibre per 100 g dry; paneer ~260 kcal/19 g protein per 100 g; besan ~22 g protein per 100 g; moong dal ~24 g protein per 100 g dry.

## Daily targets (adults)
| | Target |
|---|---|
| Calories | ~1500-1800 kcal (mild deficit) |
| Protein | 60-80 g |
| Fibre | 30 g+ |
| Salt | **under 5 g/day total (about 1 tsp), ideally 3-4 g** because of hypertension |
| Toddler | Not on a diet. Give ½ to ⅔ of an adult portion, extra ghee/paneer/curd for fat, minimal salt, mild spice |

## House rules (hypertension + weight)
1. **Salt**: tell the cook to use a measured ½ tsp salt for the whole dish serving 3, not "to taste". Use lemon, roasted jeera powder, pepper, coriander, curry leaves, ginger-garlic, amchur and kasuri methi for flavour.
2. **No pickle, papad, chutney powder, packaged sauces, instant mixes or salted nuts**. Check the sodium on the Cosmix label.
3. **Oil and ghee (revised)**: think of it as a **daily budget of about 4-5 tsp per adult** (20-25 g, the ICMR-NIN range is roughly 20-30 g), covering oil, ghee and butter across the day. Typical use by dish:
   - Chapati or dosa: ½ tsp or less per piece on a non-stick tawa.
   - Stuffed paratha: 1 tsp per paratha.
   - Gravy or sabzi (family of 3, one pot): 2-3 tsp total, so roughly 1 tsp per person.
   - Pulao or fried rice (one pot for 3): 2-3 tsp total.
   - Toddler: don't restrict fat. Add an extra ½-1 tsp of ghee to his portion.
   - Use the air fryer and a non-stick tawa, and have the cook measure with a teaspoon instead of pouring.
   - **Calorie correction**: the per-serving calories in the tables assume the leaner amounts above. If your cook uses more, add about 40 kcal for each extra tsp of oil (a tsp is about 40-45 kcal).
4. **Potassium helps BP**: tomato, beetroot, carrot, spinach, beans, dal, curd, banana. Eat them daily (unless a doctor has told the user to limit potassium).
5. **Plate method at lunch**: ½ vegetables, ¼ protein (dal, paneer, egg, soya), ¼ grain.
6. **Eggs**: 1-2 whole eggs a day is fine. Add egg whites if you want extra protein.

## Using what's already at home: isabgol and chia seeds
- **Isabgol (psyllium husk)**: adds soluble fibre, helps fullness and cholesterol. Use 1 tsp in a full glass of water once a day, or ½ tsp mixed into chilla or roti dough. Always take it with plenty of water, and keep it about 2 hours apart from BP or other medicines because it can slow their absorption. **Not for the toddler** without asking the paediatrician.
- **Chia seeds**: 1 tbsp soaked 15 minutes in water, milk or curd makes a fibre-rich pudding or smoothie booster. For the toddler, use only soaked chia in small amounts (never dry, because of choking risk).

## YOUR ROUTINES: toddler eggs, sourdough days and the freezer

### Toddler's 2 eggs a day
- Two eggs give about 12 g protein, which is already close to a toddler's daily need (about 13-15 g). So the rest of his day does not need extra protein powder or large portions of paneer or soya. Dal, milk and curd are enough.
- Rotate the format so it doesn't get boring:
  - **Veg omelette** with grated carrot, spinach or capsicum.
  - **Soft scrambled eggs** with a little butter or ghee.
  - **Banana-oat-cocoa pancakes**. Roughly 1 banana + 2 eggs + ⅓ cup oats gives about 2-3 pancakes at ~300 kcal, 15 g protein and 4 g fibre. Add 1 tsp soaked chia for fibre.
  - **Mini egg muffins** (egg + finely chopped veg, baked in the air fryer at 170 °C for 8-10 minutes). They also freeze well.
- Keep salt to a tiny pinch, and skip ketchup, cheese slices and flavoured spreads, which are salty.
- For the adults, the same banana-oat-cocoa pancake works with 1 scoop of Cosmix added, for about 350 kcal and 25 g protein.

### Sourdough days (bread + eggs, or protein shake)
**Bread + eggs plate (adult, ~350-400 kcal, ~22 g protein, 5 g fibre)**
- 2 slices sourdough (about 160-200 kcal, 7-8 g protein) + 2 eggs (omelette or bhurji with veg) + a handful of tomato and cucumber.
- **Check the sourdough label for sodium.** Bread is often 150-250 mg sodium a slice, which adds up on a low-salt plan. Keep the eggs very lightly salted and skip butter, cheese and ketchup. Use avocado, mashed beetroot-hummus or a thin layer of unsalted peanut butter instead.

**Protein shake days (adult)**
- A plain shake (milk + Cosmix) has almost no fibre and doesn't keep you full for long. Upgrade it with **¼ cup oats or 1 tbsp chia + ½ banana**, which makes it ~300 kcal, ~28 g protein and ~6 g fibre, and have 1 slice of sourdough or 1 boiled egg alongside.
- Don't use the shake as the main meal every day. Aim for at least 3 breakfasts a week from the real-food list (B1-B8, B11) so the family gets variety and vegetables.

### Freezer and air-fryer guide
You already freeze chapathis, pancakes and cutlets. This is how the recipes fit that habit.

| Recipe | Freezer-friendly? | How to freeze and reheat |
|---|---|---|
| Chapathis (plain, methi, ragi-wheat) | Yes | Stack with parchment. Reheat on a tawa or in the air fryer at 160 °C for 2-3 minutes. |
| Stuffed parathas (L12, L13) | Yes | Cook about 80%, cool, freeze flat. Reheat in the air fryer at 180 °C for 5-6 minutes. |
| Besan and moong chillas (B1, B2, S7) | Yes | Cook fully, layer with parchment. Reheat in the air fryer at 170 °C for 3-4 minutes. Add paneer in B2 after reheating if possible. |
| Banana-oat-cocoa pancakes, ragi dosa and adai | Yes | Layer with parchment. Air fry at 160 °C for 3 minutes or microwave for 30-40 seconds. |
| Cutlets (S6) | Yes | Shape, freeze raw on a tray, then bag. Air fry from frozen at 180 °C for 10-12 minutes, turning once. |
| Mini egg muffins | Yes | Microwave for 40 seconds or air fry at 160 °C for 4 minutes. |
| Rava idli (B7) and egg/paneer dosa (B6, B8) | Yes | Freeze idlis in a bag and microwave for 1 minute. Freeze dosas layered with parchment and reheat on a tawa or in the air fryer at 160 °C for 3 minutes. Cooked paneer bhurji freezes fine, but make egg dosas fresh. |
| Pulao and fried rice (L1-L3) | Yes, in portions | Cool fast, freeze in single lunch-box portions. Microwave with a splash of water. Egg fried rice is best made fresh or with the egg added fresh. |
| Dal, rajma and chana gravies (L4-L6, L9) | Yes | Freeze in 1-portion tubs. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat. |
| Paneer gravies (L7, L10) | Not ideal | Paneer turns crumbly. Freeze the gravy only and add fresh or pan-fried paneer when reheating. |
| Boiled eggs, bhurji, omelette wraps (B4, B5), chaats and raita | No | Make fresh. |
| Freezer burritos (L15) | Yes | Wrap tightly in foil or parchment, freeze up to 1 month. Microwave 2 minutes, then air fry at 180 °C for 3-4 minutes to crisp. |
| Falafel (B11) | Yes | Shape and freeze raw on a tray, then bag. Air fry from frozen at 180 °C for 12-15 minutes. Wrap fresh. |
| Lauki koftas (L14) | Yes | Freeze the air-fried koftas on a tray, then bag. Reheat in the air fryer at 180 °C for 5-6 minutes, then add to hot gravy. |
| Energy balls (S8) | Yes | Keep in the fridge for 1 week or the freezer for a month. |

**Food-safety basics**: cool food within 2 hours, freeze flat in labelled portions, use within about 1 month, and don't refreeze after thawing. Reheat until steaming hot.

### Weekly "batch cook" idea (Sunday, ~1.5 hours with the cook)
1. Roll and cook 20-25 chapathis (some methi, some ragi-wheat) and freeze.
2. Make one big batch of stuffed parathas (L12 or L13) and freeze.
3. Make cutlets (S6), egg muffins and banana-oat pancakes for the week.
4. Cook one dal or chana/rajma gravy and freeze in portions.
5. Weekday cooking then becomes only a fresh sabzi, pulao or egg dish plus reheated roti.

## Making dosa higher in protein and tastier (ready-made batter)
Plain dosa from ready-made batter is mostly rice, about 100 kcal and 2-3 g protein each. Since the batter is store-bought, the protein goes **on top of the dosa**, not into the batter.

**Protein toppings (the biggest gain)**
- **Egg dosa (B6)**: spread 1 beaten egg over the dosa while it cooks, flip briefly. Adds about 6 g protein per egg.
- **Paneer bhurji dosa (B8)**: 40-50 g crumbled paneer with onion, capsicum, tomato, turmeric and pepper. Adds about 8-9 g protein and about 100 kcal.
- **Sambar with dal and plenty of veg**: a cup adds about 4-6 g protein and fibre.

**Making it tastier without extra salt or oil**
- Cook on a hot non-stick tawa and brush with ½ tsp ghee, which gives more flavour than extra oil.
- **Chutneys**: roasted-chana mint-coriander chutney, tomato-onion-garlic chutney or a curry-leaf chutney. Go easy on coconut.
- **Homemade podi**: roast sesame, urad dal, curry leaves, red chillies and hing with just a pinch of salt. Shop-bought podi is very salty.
- Add a pinch of pepper, chopped coriander and green chilli to the egg or bhurji.
- **Check the ready-made batter label for sodium.** Many are already salted, so skip adding salt to the toppings.

## Travel-friendly snacks (dry, no fridge needed)
Good in a bag or office drawer for several hours:
- **Best travellers**: S1 roasted makhana, S10 air-fried chickpeas, S12 air-fried peas, S13 moong dal namkeen, S14 almond + seed mix, S8 energy balls, S6 cutlets and S7 mini cheela (both fine in a box for a few hours).
- **OK in a box**: S4 boiled egg + roasted chana (egg within 3-4 hours or keep it cool).
- **Best eaten fresh**: S2 sprouts chaat (goes soggy), S3 paneer tikka, S5 pepper fry, S9 corn cup and S11 tortilla pizza.
- **Almonds and the like**: 8-10 almonds is about 70-80 kcal, so measure it out. Use a small pinch of salt or none, since it adds to the daily salt limit. Whole nuts and seeds are a **choking risk for the toddler** (under 4), so give him only powdered or very finely chopped versions.

## Quick weekly rotation idea
- **Breakfast**: rotate B1, B3, B6, B4 (Mon-Thu), B7, B8, B9, B5 (Fri-Sun).
- **Lunch**: alternate a rice/millet dish (L1, L2, L3, L9), a roti dish (L4-L8, L10) and a paratha day (L12, L13), with curd rice (L11) on busy days.
- **Snack**: pick 1 per day, with at least one egg or paneer snack and one sprouts/makhana snack each week.
- **Protein check**: every day should have 2 of eggs, paneer, dal, soya, curd or milk at main meals.

## Caveats
- Nutrition values are estimates. Cooking oil, portion size and brand change them.
- This is general guidance. For hypertension and weight, follow the user's doctor's advice, especially on salt substitutes, potassium and medicine timing (isabgol).
- The toddler's menu should be checked with a paediatrician for allergies and portion sizes.
